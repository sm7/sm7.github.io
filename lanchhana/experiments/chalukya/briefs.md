# Chalukya: Pulakeśin II (reign c. 610–642 CE)

## Sources read
- Hiuen Tsiang (Xuanzang), Si-yu-ki, Book XI "Mo-ho-la-ch'a (Mahārāṣṭra)", tr. S. Beal, 1884, vol. 2 pp. 255–258. He visited the country c. 640 and names its king Pulakeśi, so he is a contemporary witness.
- Aihole inscription of Pulikeśin II, ed. and tr. F. Kielhorn, Epigraphia Indica vol. VI no. 1 (1902), pp. 1–12. Dated Śaka 556 = 634–35 CE; composed by the poet Ravikīrti, who built the Jaina temple on which it is engraved. A contemporary court poem, with the usual praise-poem exaggeration.

## Scene 1: the army on the march (Attested)
Xuanzang: the people are tall and stern, "grateful to benefactors, relentless to enemies"; they fight with lances, give warning before attacking, and do not kill one who flees; a defeated general is sent a woman's clothes; "a band of champions to the number of several hundred" who intoxicate themselves with wine before battle, one man with a lance meeting ten thousand; they beat drums before them when they go out; they also make "many hundred heads of elephants" drunk and send them rushing forward to trample the enemy; Harṣa could not subdue them.
Aihole inscription: the king's armies with elephants, banners, umbrellas and chowries; verse 24: elephants crossing the Reva (Narmadā); verse 30: he crossed the Kāverī on elephants that were rutting; the poet compares the dust of the troops to darkness with banners and chowries; verses 23, 24: Harṣa's elephants fallen in battle (Kielhorn's notes cite Raghuvaṃśa as the poet's model).
Conjecture: dress and arms, the look of the elephants' gear, the river, the numbers, the landscape. The wine and the rutting are not shown.

## Scene 2: the Jinendra temple at Aihole (Attested in the inscription)
Verses 35–37: "This stone mansion of Jinendra … has been caused to be built by the wise Ravikīrti", the poem being engraved on it (634–35 CE). The form of the temple is not described. I take it from the surviving temple at Aihole (not read in a source here).
Conjecture: everything about its shape and setting, the people, the poet, colours and the hilltop.

## Scene 3: the rock-cut monastery (Attested, Xuanzang)
On the eastern frontier "a great mountain with towering crags … a sanghārāma constructed in a dark valley; its lofty halls and deep side-aisles stretch through the face of the rocks, storey above storey, backed by the crag and facing the valley"; built by the Arhat Achara; the great vihāra about 100 feet high, in its middle a stone Buddha about 70 feet high, above it a stone canopy of seven stages "towering upwards apparently without support", about three feet between each; on the four walls, painted scenes of the Bodhisattva's earlier lives. Beal's note identifies it with the Ajanta caves (cave XXVI); he adds that the measurements "by report" are vastly exaggerated.
Conjecture: all details of form and colour, the valley, the monks. The figures are scaled by report, which Beal doubts.
