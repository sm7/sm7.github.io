# Chola: Rājarāja I's Tanjore (reign 985–1014 CE)

Source read: South-Indian Inscriptions vol. II, "Tamil Inscriptions of Rajaraja, Rajendra-Chola and others in the Rajarajesvara temple at Tanjavur", ed. and tr. E. Hultzsch / V. Venkayya (1913). These are the temple's own records, cut on its walls, so they are contemporary primary sources (dated to Rājarāja's regnal years, up to the 29th, c. 1014 CE; a few later Chola and Nayaka records are mixed in).

## Scene 1: the temple (Rājarājeśvara)
Attested in the inscriptions: a gilded copper pot for the pinnacle "placed on the temple of the lord" (458½ kalañju by the Āḍavallān weight; No. 89); a second gopura and an enclosure with pillars (records cut "on a pillar of the west enclosure", "in the second gopura", "outside of the north enclosure"); seven copper images of Gaṇapati, two in the dancing posture (Nos. 84–85); gilt copper pots, large silver dishes of 300–590 kalañju, jewelled ornaments from the treasure taken from the Cheras and Pāṇḍyas, pearls poured as flowers at the god's feet (Nos. 90–93); lamps kept burning on ghee supplied by shepherds from 48 cows or 96 ewes each (No. 94).
Not in this source: the form of the stone tower itself. It stands today; I take its stepped form from the surviving monument, not from the inscriptions.
Conjecture: colours, plaster and paint, crowd, dress, time of day, cloister and gopura details.

## Scene 2: the streets outside Tanjore (Attested, No. 94, Venkayya's note)
Ten streets "outside" (purambadi) Tanjore: Gandharva-teru (musicians), Villigal-teru (bowmen), Anaikkaduvar-teru (those who cook for elephants), Anaiyatkal-teru (elephant troops), Panmaiyar-teru, Madaippalli-teru (adjoining the temple kitchen), and four named for the Chola family (Virasērapperunderu, Rājavidyādharapperunderu, Jayangondasērapperunderu, Sīrasikhāmaṇipperunderu); an eleventh street within the town, Sāliyat-teru (weavers). Three bazaars outside (Tribhuvanamadevi-perangadi, Kongavalar-angadi and another). The shepherds living there kept cows, she-buffaloes and ewes.
Not attested: house form, street layout, paving, dress, the look of the bazaars.
Conjecture: all of the architecture and the crowd; the elephants are inferred from the street names.

## Second source: S. R. Balasubrahmanyam, Middle Chola Temples (Rajaraja I to Kulottunga I), 1975 (secondary)
Archive.org copy read: pp. 16–27 (the temple and its precinct), p. 74 and pp. 81–82 (streets). He cites the inscriptions, the Tiruvisaippa of Karuvur Devar (Rajarāja's guru) and the Tanjore District Gazetteer.
- Temple faces east. Approaching from the east: a deep uneven moat, filled at the entrance for a causeway; beyond it a wall of fortification with bastions along the moat; 6.1 m inside it a more massive enclosure wall; over its east opening the outer gopura, the Keralāntakan tiruvāsal, "massive", entablature plain, superstructure "stocky and short"; about 100 m further west the inner gopura, the Rājarājan tiruvāsal, with panels on its basement; then the courtyard with the main temple, cloistered (Kṛṣṇan Rāman tiruccuṟṟumāḷigai), eight dikpāla shrines, Caṇḍeśvara shrine. Moat and bastioned wall are in the Tiruvisaippa, which calls the city "Iñji-sūl Tañjai", Tanjavur surrounded by a moat, so they are original.
- The śrīvimāna is pyramidal, 13 storeys (talas), 63.4 m (1975 remeasurement) over a double-storeyed sanctum, 30.18 m square at the base; a single granite block 7.77 m square (about 80 tons) at the top with a pair of Nandis at each corner; a 25-ton cupola-shaped śikhara; a gilded stūpi gifted in Rājarāja's 25th year.
- The palace lay north of the temple complex ("the temple served as chapel-royal for the royal family whose palace was in its vicinity"). The north entrance of the cloister wall was perhaps used by royalty. An inscription (SII II) was ordered from the royal bathing hall (tirumañjanaśālai) lying east of the hall of Irumaḍi Cholan in the palace at Tanjavur.
- In Rājarāja's 29th year two new temple streets, the south and north Taḷic-cēri, each in two rows of about a hundred houses a row, were built to house the temple's women servants.
- Streets and bazaars as in the inscriptions (ten outside, weavers' street inside, bazaars outside the city limits).
Not attested anywhere read: the palace's architecture, the town's plan, its gates or any wall around the town itself, any water other than the moat.

## Scene 3: the city overview (hypothetical)
Attested (second source, citing the inscriptions and Tiruvisaippa): the moated, bastioned temple precinct facing east; the palace to the north of it; two temple streets of about 400 houses in all; the town ringed by a moat; streets and bazaars outside it.
Conjecture: the palace's form and size, the layout and extent of the town and its moat, house forms, the spacing between the streets, the surrounding fields.
