# Gupta: Pāṭaliputra under Candragupta II, c. 405–411 CE

Source read verbatim: Faxian (Fa-hien), Record of Buddhistic Kingdoms, tr. Legge 1886, ch. XXVII (archive.org recordofbuddhist00fahsuoft). Eyewitness, in Candragupta II's reign (Faxian does not name the king). Faxian gives no walls, streets or layout of the city, so there is no honest "city plan" scene; the three scenes below are what he does describe.

## Scene 1: Image procession (Attested)
Yearly on the 8th day of the 2nd month: a four-wheeled car carrying a five-storeyed structure of bamboos tied together, supported by a king-post with slanting poles and lances, a little over 20 cubits high, tope-shaped. Wrapped in white silk-like cloth, painted in various colours. Figures of devas in gold, silver and lapis lazuli, silken streamers and canopies. Niches on the four sides, each with a seated Buddha and a standing Bodhisattva. About twenty cars, each different. Monks and laity, singers, skilful musicians, flowers and incense; Brahmans invite the Buddhas to enter the city; lamps burn all night.
Conjecture: street architecture, dress, the colours painted, crowd layout.

## Scene 2: Monastery beside Aśoka's tope (Attested, thin)
By the side of the tope of Aśoka a very grand and beautiful Mahāyāna monastery and a Hīnayāna one, with 600–700 monks together. The great tope is more than 3 li south of the city, with a Buddha footprint and a vihāra before it. The palace halls were "carved and inlaid" stone, said to be spirit-made, still standing.
Conjecture: the tope's shape and size, monastery plan, brick/stone, colours.

## Scene 3: House of charity (Attested)
Heads of Vaiśya families set up houses in the cities giving charity and medicine. The poor, orphans, widowers, childless, maimed, cripples and sick go there; doctors examine, food and medicines are given, and they leave when better.
Conjecture: building form, costume, furnishing.
