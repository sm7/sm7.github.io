# Hoysala: Viṣṇuvardhana (reign c. 1110–1152 CE) at Velāpura (Belūr)

## Source read
B. L. Rice, Mysore Inscriptions, translated for Government (Bangalore, 1879), pp. 264–269, no. 145 (Śaka 1039 = 1117 CE, Belūr, copper-plate and stone grants of Viṣṇuvardhana Poysala Deva to the god Vijaya Nārāyaṇa); archive.org/details/mysoreinscripti00ricegoog. Translation, with Rice's note that the similes are puns.
- Viṣṇuvardhana, with his chief queen Śāntala Devī ("a crowning ornament of finished dancers"), "from his palace in the great city of Velapura was ruling the kingdom in peace and wisdom".
- The temple of Vijaya Nārāyaṇa is "a lofty pile, adorned with all the signs of fortune", "filled with pillars", "being filled with lions and elephants (among the decorations)", with large chambers, deep and peaceful interior, "adorned with numerous gilded pinnacles, towering up in innumerable storeys" (poetic exaggeration).
- Endowment: daily service, divine decoration and ceremonies of the three watches of the gods Vijaya Nārāyaṇa, Channa Keśava, Lakṣmī Nārāyaṇa; support of Vaishnavas, Brahmans, learned men to write the prayers and songs, garland-makers, dancing girls for illumination (lamps), and all kinds of temple servants; lands granted to 120 Bhaṭṭas, 21 Bhaṭṭas, 32 Vaishnavas.
- A later grant on the same pages (Vīra Ballāḷa's time, c. 1200, the Belur inscription of the Tamra Śāsana) praises the city/temple precinct: "his doors securely fastened, his lodges filled with guards and archers, his ramparts high, surrounded with a moat — the lake named Vasudeva tīrtha". Not Viṣṇuvardhana's own time. Treat as a later witness.
- Not read: any source for the architectural form (the surviving Channakeśava temple at Belur).

## Scenes
1. The temple: the lofty pillared stone shrine with lions and elephants in the decoration and gilded pinnacles (Attested as a poem). Conjecture: all form; "innumerable storeys" is poetic.
2. Daily service: dancing girls with lamps, garland-makers, scribes of prayers and songs, Vaishnava priests, three images. Conjecture: setting, dress, instruments.
3. The city of Velapura with high ramparts, moat, guards and archers (Later witness).
## Dress rule (Chola review): no stitched blouses/draped saris, no turbans, no modern dance costume, no machine-made tiles.
