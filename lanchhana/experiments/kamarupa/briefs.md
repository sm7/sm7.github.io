# Kāmarūpa: Bhāskaravarman "Kumāra" (reign c. 600–650 CE)

## Source read
- Xuanzang, Si-yu-ki, Book X, tr. S. Beal, 1884, vol. 2 pp. 195–198 (contemporary witness, c. 643 CE).
  Kamarupa about 10,000 li round; capital about 30 li; land low but rich and regularly cultivated; jackfruit (Panasa) and coconut (Nārikela) trees numerous and valued; water led from the river or banked-up lakes flows round the towns; soft, temperate climate; people simple and honest, small in stature, complexion dark yellow, speech slightly different from Mid-India, impetuous, retentive memories, earnest in study; they worship the Devas and have no faith in Buddha, no saṅghārāma ever built; about 100 Deva temples and several myriads of sectaries; the king, of the old line of Nārāyaṇa-deva, a Brahman named Bhāskaravarman with title Kumāra; fond of learning; invited Xuanzang three times, who came at Śīlabhadra's urging; Kumāra-rāja received him, saying he had long loved men of learning, and spoke of songs about the Tsin king of Mahāchīna.

## Scene 1: the capital (Attested): water channels round the town, low fertile land, jackfruit and coconut, Deva temples (form not described). Conjecture: architecture, layout, the town's look, dress.
