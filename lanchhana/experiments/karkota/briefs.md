# Karkoṭa: Lalitāditya-Muktāpīḍa's foundations (c. 725–760 CE)

Source read verbatim: Kalhaṇa, Rājataraṅgiṇī IV.192–204, tr. M. A. Stein 1900 (vol. 1 pp. 141–42, with Stein's notes). Kalhaṇa wrote c. 1148, about four centuries later, from tradition; Stein's note gives the standing ruins of the Mārtāṇḍa temple (an archaeological check). No contemporary description of a Karkoṭa city, so no city or street scene.

## Scene 1: Mārtāṇḍa temple (Attested: text and ruins)
Text: "the wonderful [shrine] of Mārtāṇḍa, with its massive walls of stone within a lofty enclosure (prāsādāntar)" and a town "swelling with grapes" (IV.192). Stein's note: the ruins "consist of a lofty central edifice, with a small detached shrine on either side of the entrance, and of a quadrangular courtyard of imposing dimensions surrounded by colonnades"; on the northern edge of the arid plateau (udar) of Mattan; a spring tīrtha nearby.
Conjecture: roof form, ornament, the colonnade's design, colours, people, season.

## Scene 2: Parihāsapura (Attested in Kalhaṇa, no remains described)
Lalitāditya built the town Parihāsapura (IV.194). Silver image of Parihāsakeśava; a golden Viṣṇu Muktākeśava (84,000 tolas of gold); golden-armoured Mahāvarāha; a silver Govardhanadhara; a great stone pillar 54 hands high topped by a Garuḍa; the Rājavihāra with a large quadrangle (catuḥśāla), a large caitya and a large Jina image; the copper "Great Buddha" reaching up to the sky (IV.195–203).
Conjecture: all architecture, scale of the copper Buddha (poetic), layout, dress, colours.
