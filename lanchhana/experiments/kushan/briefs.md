# Kushan: Kanishka's stūpa at Puruṣapura (Peshawar)

One scene only. No source describes a Kushan city or street. Both texts below were written 3–5 centuries after Kanishka and carry legend.

Sources read verbatim:
- Faxian (c. 400), tr. Legge 1886, ch. XII: a tope "more than four hundred cubits high, adorned with layers of all the precious substances"; of all topes and temples seen, none comparable "in solemn beauty and majestic grandeur"; a small tope (the herd-boy's) came out of its side on the south, a little over three cubits high.
- Xuanzang (643), tr. Beal 1884, Book II, Gandhara: a stone stūpa on the little stūpa's site, 400 ft high, base circumference 1½ li, five storeys each 150 ft; 25 gilded copper circlets on a staff on top; a peck of relics placed in the middle; the small stūpa at the south-east of the base, projecting about half-way up.

Attested (by these later pilgrims): stone; five storeys; vast base; 25 gilded rings on a staff; small stūpa at SE of the base.
Heights (400 ft / 400 cubits, 150 ft per storey) are legendary and not rendered literally.
Conjecture: tier proportions, Gandharan ornament, surrounding monastery, dress, landscape.
