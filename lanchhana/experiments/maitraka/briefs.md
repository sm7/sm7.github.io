# Maitraka of Valabhī: the king Xuanzang met, c. 640 CE (Dhruvapata)

## Source read
Xuanzang, Si-yu-ki, Book XI, tr. S. Beal, 1884, vol. 2 pp. 266–268 (contemporary witness), with Beal's notes. The king's name "Dhruvapata" is identified by Bühler and Cunningham with Dhruvabhaṭa (Śīlāditya VI), by Burgess with Dhruvasena II, by Oldenberg possibly with Dherabhaṭa: the identification is disputed.

Valabhi: country about 6000 li round, capital about 30 li; like Malava in soil, climate and manners; population very dense, establishments rich; some hundred families possess a hundred lakhs; rare and valuable products of distant regions stored in great quantities; about a hundred sangharamas with 6000 priests (mostly Little Vehicle, Sammatiya school); several hundred Deva temples with many sectaries. The king: a Kshattriya, nephew of Śīlāditya of Malava, son-in-law of Harṣa's son (the Kanyakubja king); quick and hasty in temper, shallow in statecraft; recently a sincere Buddhist; yearly he summons a great assembly and for seven days gives away gems, exquisite meats, the three garments and medicines to priests, and costly articles of the seven precious substances, then redeems them at twice their price; he esteems virtue and honours learned priests from distant regions. Not far from the city a great sangharama built by the Arhat Achara, where Gunamati and Sthiramati lived and wrote treatises.

## Scene 1: the merchant city (Attested): wealth, dense population, stored goods from distant lands, monasteries and temples. Conjecture: layout, architecture, dress.
## Scene 2: the seven-day assembly (Attested): gifts to monks, redeemed at twice their price. Conjecture: setting, dress, the king's look, the pavilion.

P. Ray, Ars Orientalis 34, 2004); Maitraka silver coins and copper coins with a trident and often a war-axe; Dudda-Vihara built for Dudda (niece of Dhruvasena I); Dhruvasena II (627–641) donated to Buddhist and Brahmanical institutions; I-tsing ranks Vallabhi with Nalanda; excavations by M. S. University of Baroda 1979–80 (R. N. Mehta). The article cites the Maitraka copper plates and CII vol. 4.
