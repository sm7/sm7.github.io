# Maurya capital experiment: Pāṭaliputra (Palibothra) in the reign of Candragupta Maurya, c. 321–297 BCE

Era fixed by the user: Candragupta, not Aśoka. Megasthenes, Seleucus I's ambassador at this court, is the contemporary witness behind Strabo and Diodorus here. Aśokan edicts, Faxian and the Kukkuṭārāma monastery are later than this era and are out of scope.

Status: source gathering. No images generated yet.

## Sources actually read (LacusCurtius, Loeb texts)
- Strabo, Geography 15.1.35-36, 15.1.50-51, 15.1.55 (after Megasthenes): Palibothra at the confluence of the Ganges and another river; 80 stades long, 15 wide; parallelogram; wooden palisade with loopholes for archers; ditch for defence and as a sewer; commissioners (astynomoi) in groups of five over city, markets, harbours, temples (verify exact wording against the Loeb text before publication).
- Diodorus Siculus 2.39.3: Palibothra "most renowned and largest" city, costly palace, ditches filled from the river (a mythic founder attribution, Heracles; use only for ditches and palace).

## Not read, so not used
- Arrian, Indica 10 (570 towers, 64 gates): host unreachable.
- Aśokan inscriptions, Arthaśāstra 2.3-4, Faxian, Xuanzang: hosts unreachable (Gutenberg, Wikisource pages blocked or absent).

## Element labels
City view: ATTESTED timber palisade with loopholes, water-filled ditch, long narrow riverside plan, confluence of two rivers. INFERRED wood and brick building, thatch and tile roofs. CONJECTURAL everything else.
Street: ATTESTED officials supervising markets, foreigners looked after. No physical description survives in the read sources: street fabric is CONJECTURAL.
Religious site: NO read source describes the appearance of any Pāṭaliputra shrine or vihāra. "Temple" is anachronistic for the Mauryan city; the Buddhist foundation traditionally tied to Aśoka there (Kukkuṭārāma) is known from later texts not yet read. Any image would be wholly conjectural.

## Now read: Arthaśāstra 2.3 and 2.4 (Shamasastry 1915 trans., supplied by the user; pp. 66-73)
Status of the text: prescriptive, attributed to Candragupta's minister Kauṭilya, date of the text debated. Everything below is PRESCRIBED, not observed.
- 2.3 fort: capital at a river confluence; three ditches (14, 12, 10 dandas wide; dandas = 6 ft), brick or stone sides, water, crocodiles and lotus; rampart 6 dandas high of heaped earth; brick parapets; chariot passage of palm trunks or stone slabs, "never of wood" (fire); square towers with ladders; between towers a broad roofed street; archers' galleries of planks (Indrakosa); gate with ornamental arch (toranasirah), turrets, alligator-faced gate turret.
- 2.4 inside the fort: three royal roads west-east and three south-north; twelve gates; royal roads and chariot roads 4 dandas (24 ft) wide; palace north of centre, facing north or east; merchants of scents, garlands, grain, liquids on the east; shops and hospitals north-west; Brahmans and the city's tutelary deity to the north; "in the centre of the city, the apartments of Gods": Aparājita, Apratihata, Jayanta, Vaijayanta, Śiva, Vaiśravaṇa, the Aśvins and the liquor-house; guardian deities at corners; shrines and places of pilgrimage 100 bows outside the ditch; a well for every ten houses.
