# Mysore: Seringapatam (Srirangapatna) in the time of Hyder Ali and Tipu Sultan, as seen in 1800

Source read verbatim: Francis Buchanan, A Journey from Madras through the Countries of Mysore, Canara and Malabar (1807), vol. I ch. II, pp. 58–74 (archive.org journeyfrommadra01hami_2). Eyewitness, May 1800, a year after Tipu's fall; he describes the buildings as he found them. He is hostile to Tipu; descriptions of the buildings are plain.

## Scene 1: City (Attested)
Island about 3 miles by 1 between branches of the Cauvery (a large rapid river with a wide rocky channel, fordable in May). The fort at the upper end: "an immense, unfinished... mass of building", naked rock and dirty mud walls; the old Indian style: long straight walls, square bastions, cavaliers heaped one above another, ditches cut through the granite; glacis high and steep; a canal parallel to the works for a garden; inner rampart and a wide deep inner ditch. A bridge on the south branch of square granite pillars carrying long stones, with an aqueduct canal bringing water into the town. Streets narrower and more confused than anywhere since Bengal; houses mostly mean. The Śrī Raṅga temple (Viṣṇu) is of great antiquity.
Conjecture: the temple's gopura in view, boats, colours, the people.

## Scene 2: Laul Bagh palace and Hyder's mausoleum (Attested)
At the lower end of the island, outside the walls: the Laul Bagh palace, built of mud yet of considerable elegance, "the handsomest native building that I have ever seen"; the garden laid out at expense with canals from the river; near it the mausoleum of Hyder, where Tipu also lies; ornamented with columns of black hornblende that takes a splendid polish; tombs covered with rich cloths; mullahs praying and musicians performing the nobat. The Dariya Daulat Bagh palace nearby has walls covered with paintings of processions of Hyder and Tipu, the defeat of Colonel Baillie, and local costumes.
Conjecture: dome and plan, colours, planting, figures.

No temple scene: Buchanan does not describe the Śrī Raṅga temple's form. No street scene beyond "narrow and mean".
