# Pallava: Narasimhavarman I Mamalla's Kāñcī and Māmallapuram (reign c. 630–668 CE)

## Scene 1: Kāñcīpuram as seen c. 640 (Attested, contemporary visitor)
Source read: Hiuen Tsiang (Xuanzang), Si-yu-ki, Book X "Dravida", tr. S. Beal, 1884, vol. 2 pp. 228–229 (read in the archive.org copy). Xuanzang's account is c. 646; he gives the capital of Dravida, Kāñcīpura (Kin-chi-pu-lo), "about 30 li round"; the soil fertile and regularly cultivated, much grain, "many flowers and fruits", precious gems; hot climate; the people courageous, honest, and esteeming learning, with a script close to Mid-India's; "some hundred" sanghārāmas and 10,000 priests of the Sthavira school of the Great Vehicle; "some eighty Deva temples" and many Nirgranthas (naked Jain ascetics); stūpas built by Aśoka over places the Buddha had visited; the birthplace of the Buddhist teacher Dharmapāla.
Not given: the form of any building, a street, a wall, the palace, the king.
Conjecture: all architecture (brick, timber and stucco, little of it surviving), dress, layout, the proportion of Buddhist, Jain and Hindu buildings in view.

## Scene 2: Māmallapuram (Attested in inscriptions and the surviving monument)
Source read: South-Indian Inscriptions vol. I, ed. and tr. E. Hultzsch, 1890, Nos. 1–23 at Māmallapuram. The Dharmarāja Ratha carries 17 inscriptions of birudas of "Narasimha" in an archaic alphabet, cut on its first, second and third storeys, and a later king, Atyantakāma, claimed it and named it Atyantakāma-Pallaveśvara-gṛha; other temples at the site are called after the same king (Hultzsch pp. 1–4). The inscriptions are lists of titles and temple-founding verses, not descriptions.
From the surviving monuments (not read in a source here): the Dharmarāja Ratha is a monolithic, three-storeyed pyramidal temple cut from a granite outcrop.
Conjecture: the sculptors at work, the surrounding site, tools, colours and plaster.
Not shown: the Shore Temple (later, under Rājasiṃha, c. 700).
