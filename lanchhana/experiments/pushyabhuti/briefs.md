# Pushyabhuti: Kanyakubja (Kanauj) under Harṣa, c. 643 CE

Source read verbatim: Xuanzang, Records of Western Countries (Beal, Si-yu-ki, 1884), Book V, Kanyakubja, vol. I pp. 206–220 (archive.org siyukibuddhistre01hsua_0). Eyewitness (Xuanzang was at the 643 assembly). Not yet read: Bāṇa, Harṣacarita.

## City (Attested)
Capital on the west, bordering the Ganges, c. 20 li long, 4–5 li broad. Dry ditch round it, strong lofty towers facing one another. Flowers and woods, lakes and ponds "bright and pure and shining like mirrors" on every side. Valuable merchandise; people well off, houses rich. c. 100 sanghārāmas with 10,000 monks; 200 deva temples.
Second source (Beal's note, from Cunningham): the surviving site is triangular, each side covered by a ditch or dry nala.
Conjecture: wall material, tower form, house style, roof colour, dress.

## Street (Attested): Harṣa's procession to the assembly
Highly decorated pavilions and musicians stationed all along from the travelling palace to the sanghārāma. A golden Buddha about 3 ft high on a gorgeously caparisoned elephant, raised aloft. On its left Harṣa dressed as Śakra holding a precious canopy; on the right Kumāra-rāja as Brahmā with a white chāmara. 500 armoured war-elephants escort each. 100 great elephants carry musicians (drums) before and behind. Harṣa scatters pearls, gold and silver flowers.
Conjecture: street architecture, dress cuts, elephant trappings, crowd.

## Temple (Attested): sanghārāma and tower at Kanyakubja
A great sanghārāma on the west bank of the Ganges; east of it a "precious tower" c. 100 ft high with a golden Buddha statue, as tall as the king, in the middle; south of the tower a precious altar for washing the image; a pavilion over the gate of the sanghārāma; a flowery copse.
Conjecture: form of the tower (stūpa vs multi-storey), brick/stucco, colours.
