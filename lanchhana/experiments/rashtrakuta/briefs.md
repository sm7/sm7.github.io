# Rāṣṭrakūṭa ("Balhara" in Arab sources): Amoghavarṣa I's time (reign c. 814–878 CE)

## Sources read
- Sulaymān the merchant (851 CE) in "Early Arab Geographers", in H. M. Elliot, History of India as told by its own historians vol. 1 (ed. Dowson, 1867), pp. 3–5 and nearby: the Balhara is "the most eminent of the princes of India"; all princes pay homage to him; his envoys are received with profound respect; he pays his troops regularly "as the practice is among the Arabs"; many horses and elephants and immense wealth; Tatariya dirhams; kings reign long; he favours the Arabs more than any other Indian king; kingdom begins at the Konkan coast.
- Ibn Ḥawqal / Iṣṭakhrī (same volume): the Balhara's city is Mankir; Muslims live in the cities of his realm, there are Jāmiʿ mosques, and only Muslims rule them on his behalf. Al-Masʿūdī (332 AH = 943–44): Mankir is "the great centre of India". Elliot notes Mankir's site is uncertain.
- Not read: any Indian-language inscription of the dynasty (Kailāsa temple at Ellora is NOT shown: no source read for it).
