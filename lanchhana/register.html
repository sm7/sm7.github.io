Critical edition and vulgate

Verse numbers follow the Bhandarkar Oriental Research Institute's critical edition of the Mahābhārata, in the electronic text by Muneo Tokunaga and John D. Smith. K. M. Ganguli's English translation follows the older vulgate, so its section numbers differ and it contains passages the critical edition sets aside.

The best-known list of Pāṇḍava banners, with Bhīma's silver lion and Yudhiṣṭhira's moon, is one of those passages. Those cards are tagged vulgate and quote Ganguli instead of the Sanskrit. The Rāmāyaṇa card uses the vulgate numbering of valmikiramayan.net.

Left out, and why

  • The Draupadeyas. Ganguli says their standards bore images of Dharma, Marut, Śakra and the Aśvins, but not which son carried which. The pairing would be a guess.
  • The Pāṇḍya king's standard. Ganguli names him Sāraṅgadhvaja but describes no emblem.
  • Royal lines of the Purāṇas. The Purāṇas list kings of Magadha and of the solar and lunar houses, but they give no emblems.

How to read the evidence tag

SealEmblem on the royal seal of copper-plate or clay-seal grants. This is the formal lāñchhana and the strongest evidence.
CoinRecurring coin type. It shows what the dynasty put on its money, not a declared insignia.
MonumentNo dynastic seal exists. A monument stands for the dynasty by convention.
TraditionNamed as the emblem in later writing, but not found on the dynasty's own seals or coins.

Left out, and why

  • No verified emblem. Shunga, Vakataka, Later Guptas of Magadha, Utpala of Kashmir, Shailodbhava, Bhaumakara, Somavamshi, early Kalachuri of Mahishmati, Kalabhra. No source describes a fixed seal emblem for these dynasties, so none is drawn.
  • After 1040 CE. The Sena, Gāhaḍavāla, Hoysala, Kākatīya, Vijayanagara, Chandela, Chaulukya and Chauhan emblems are on the Later kingdoms tab.
  • Seal emblem uncertain. Gurjara-Pratīhāra seals are said to show the goddess Bhagavatī, but no reliable account of her iconography was found, so the card shows Mihira Bhoja's Ādivarāha coins.

Flags, and when they date from

A small flag beside a kingdom's name is drawn from the best description of that flag, and its tag says how well the flag itself is attested. Many Rajput flags are known only from the princely era. The coats of arms with mottoes and supporters belong to the British-era armorial tradition of the late 19th century and are not shown as royal insignia.

Left out, and why

  • Gajapati of Odisha. No coins are known and no seal emblem was found; the inscriptions give the title, not an emblem.
  • Kalachuri of Kalyāṇi. A golden bull is named in secondary works, but no seal or coin showing it was found.
  • Koch (Cooch Behar). Their Narayani coins carry legends only, and no pictorial emblem, seal emblem or flag is known.
  • Manipur. The Pakhangba flag is documented only from 1947–49.
